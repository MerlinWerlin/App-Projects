Satchel Privacy Policy

Last updated: 1 November 2026

Data Not Collected

In short: Satchel has no accounts, no servers of ours, no ads, no analytics and no tracking. Your trips, tickets, documents, notes and emergency card are saved on your phone. We never see them. A few optional features look things up online; each one is listed below with exactly what it sends and to whom.

What stays on your phone

  • Trips, bookings, timelines, saved places, ideas, packing lists, spending and your settings.
  • Tickets, PDFs, images and receipts you attach, and any QR code crops you save. Satchel keeps its own copy on your phone; your originals in Files or Photos are not changed.
  • Document vault copies (for example passport, visa or licence copies) and their expiry dates.
  • Your emergency card, help cards and trusted contacts: insurance details, contacts you pick, and private medical notes and medicine times.
  • Your trip journal, photos and other personal notes (for example hotel door codes, home Wi-Fi or a sitter note).
  • Offline city maps you download, and the last weather forecast, so they work in flight mode.

None of this is sent to us. If you delete Satchel, this data is deleted from your phone with it, unless it is in your own phone backup or a Satchel backup file you saved.

Location

Satchel never tracks you in the background and keeps no location history. It asks for your location only when you use a feature that needs it:

  • Where am I? on the map, Find toilets near me or Find help near me, starting a day plan or setting a spot from where you are, and Share where I am: one check, then it stops.
  • Find my way back: your position and compass heading are followed only while that screen is open, and stop as soon as you leave it.

Your location is not sent to us. It leaves your phone only in two cases you start yourself: a nearby toilet or help search with no offline city map for that area (see OpenStreetMap below), or a message you choose to send from Share where I am.

Online services Satchel uses

Satchel works without a connection. When you are online, these features contact other companies' services directly from your phone. They are third parties with their own privacy policies; like any website, they can see your phone's internet (IP) address. Satchel never sends them your name, account, trips, documents or contacts.

  • The map (Apple Maps on iPhone, maps.apple.com). The map background is your phone's built-in map service, which loads map images for the area you are looking at. Tapping Open in Apple Maps, directions or an address opens the Apple Maps app with that place (and your starting point, if Satchel has one). Apple's privacy policy applies. (On Android, the system map is Google Maps.)
  • Weather (MET Norway, api.met.no). To show a forecast on a trip, Satchel sends the coordinates of that trip's hotel or saved place (rounded to about 10 metres) to the Norwegian Meteorological Institute. Not your own location. The last forecast is kept on your phone.
  • Toilets and help nearby (OpenStreetMap Overpass, overpass-api.de). If no offline city map covers the spot, Satchel sends that spot (your current location or your hotel's, whichever you searched from) and a search radius, and gets back nearby public places from OpenStreetMap.
  • Offline city maps (GitHub, github.com). When you open the city map list or download a city, Satchel fetches the list and the map file from GitHub, where the maps are published. The request names the file, not where you are.
  • App updates (Expo). Satchel may check Expo's update service for a fix to the app. The request carries technical details such as the app version and platform, not your data.
  • Links you choose to open in your browser. Source links on emergency numbers, customs and other guides, and links you save on ideas, open in your browser only when you tap them. That website's own policy applies.

Photos, files and contacts

Satchel only reads a photo, a file or a contact when you pick it, and only uses the camera when you choose to take a photo. Picked photos and files are copied into Satchel on your phone. Only the contacts you choose are saved, on your phone. Satchel does not upload your photo library, files or address book. Text is read from the clipboard only when you tap Paste.

Face ID and passcode

The document vault, and hotel door or key box codes you choose to hide, can be locked with Face ID, Touch ID or your passcode. Your phone handles this check. Satchel only learns whether it succeeded; your face data, fingerprint and passcode never leave your phone and are never seen by Satchel.

Backups, trip files and sharing

Satchel never uploads a backup. A backup is a file you save through the share sheet (for example to Files, iCloud Drive or AirDrop), and you decide where it goes. It includes your vault documents only if you choose Include documents. Backup files are not encrypted, so keep them somewhere private.

A trip file you send to someone contains that trip: its bookings and their attached files, places and packing list (journal photos only if you include them). It never contains your vault documents, emergency card, medical notes, home Wi-Fi or alarm codes. Calendar files, shared itineraries, messages from Share where I am and anything else you share go only where you send them.

Purchases

If you buy the optional one-time upgrade (Satchel Plus), the purchase is handled by Apple (or Google Play on Android). We do not receive your payment details, your name or your Apple ID. The app store only tells Satchel that the upgrade is unlocked.

Sync across your devices

Satchel does not sync. To move to a new phone, save a backup file and open it there. If optional sync is ever added, this policy will be updated before that happens.

Children

Satchel does not collect personal information from anyone, including children. Details you add about children on a trip stay on your phone.
