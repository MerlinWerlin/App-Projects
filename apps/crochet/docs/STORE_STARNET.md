DRAFT 1
Title: A simple way I keep track of amigurumi increases

When I’m shaping a ball, I find it easier to write down the round structure before I start: how many plain rounds, where the increases happen, and the stitch total after each increase round. After each round, I check the total before moving on. It’s a small pause that can catch a missed increase early.

I’m the maker of Rowlight, and I built an even-increase tool for this kind of planning. But paper notes work just as well. Curious what systems other amigurumi makers use to keep track of shaping?

DRAFT 2
Title: How do you keep your place in a written pattern?

A few things that help me: mark the current step before setting a project down, keep repeat counts separate from row counts, and check the stitch total at the end of a shaping row. If you use a printed pattern, a ruler or bookmark can also make it easier to return to the right line.

I’m the maker of Rowlight, a crochet pattern companion, and I’ve been thinking a lot about this problem while making it. What’s your go-to method—paper, notes on your phone, stitch markers, or something else?

DRAFT 3
Title: A quick gauge reminder for anyone starting a new project

Gauge can change with the yarn, hook, and how you crochet, so it’s worth making a swatch with the materials you plan to use. Measure the stitches and rows over the pattern’s stated area, rather than relying only on how the fabric looks. If your gauge differs, check the pattern’s guidance before changing hook size or yarn.

I’m the maker of Rowlight, which includes a gauge tool, and wanted to share the reminder because I’ve rushed past swatching myself. Do you usually swatch, or only when fit or finished size is especially important?
