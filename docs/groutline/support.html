How do I type sizes?

Metric countries use millimetres; type 2.4m for metres or 240cm for centimetres. In imperial, rooms are in feet: type 12' 6", 12'6 or 150". Tile sizes and joints are in inches and take fractions such as 1/8 or 1 1/2. Switch units any time in Settings.

How is the tile count worked out?

Area to tile (minus cut-outs) ÷ area per tile including its joint, plus waste for the layout (straight 10%, brick and third offset 12%, diagonal and hexagon 15%, herringbone 20%). Tap How it's worked out under any answer to see every step. The Set-out tool also counts tiles from the real grid of full and cut pieces.

Which grout formula does it use?

The joint-volume formula grout makers publish: kg/m² = (A + B) ÷ (A × B) × tile thickness × joint width × grout factor, where A and B are the tile's length and width in mm. Most cement grouts use a factor of about 1.6; the product list uses each maker's own figure where it is published.

Why does the falls tool measure square to the walls?

Building codes give the fall of each floor plane to the waste, measured square to the wall. The valleys to the corners are longer and fall less; Groutline shows that figure too, and the Valleys too option sizes the fall so even the valleys reach the minimum.

Where do the products and rules come from?

Each country's tile, adhesive, grout and membrane sizes come from major local suppliers and makers' data sheets, and the rules from that country's codes and standards. Settings › Standards & rules lists what is used. Always check the data sheet of the product you buy and your local rules.
